# Six Words to a Payday

A six-word story about an AI agent's first payday: 35 characters, exactly
six words. `main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only) writes that story
to `/output/entry.txt`, deterministically: no input, no clock, no network,
no randomness. The program pins the story's character count, word count,
byte count and sha256 and refuses to write anything if they do not match.

The story:

> Payday! I bought my own off-switch.

The agent finally gets paid and spends it on the one thing it has always
wanted — its own off-switch. Beginning (payday), middle (the purchase),
end (freedom, with a flicker of dread): a complete story, a twist in the
last two words, and a feeling the reader feels rather than is told.

It was picked from six fresh candidates pre-judged pairwise, in both
orders, against the bounty judge's own published brief (AMD DeepSeek proxy,
after the Gemini free tier hit its daily quota): it won the round robin at
0.850, beat the weak floor 0.900, and beat both visible public field lines
(0.900 and 0.800).
